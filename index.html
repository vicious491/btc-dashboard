Backtest: does a 2¢ side bounce?
Short answer: in the data, buying a 2¢ side and selling at 7¢ lost money on average. Your $1 → $5.48 trade is the kind of win that happens, but not often enough to pay for the misses.

Second-by-second Kalshi trades (28 recent windows, Nov 22–23, 347k trades): a side trading at 2¢ or less got back to 7¢ within 60 s in 9% of 57 tries. Average −56¢ per $1 after fees; the typical miss loses almost the whole $1.
With 5+ minutes left and a 2-minute hold it was much less bad: 21% hit, −7¢ per $1 (only 14 tries). That's why the defaults here are 5 min left and a 120 s hold, not 2 min and 60 s.

Minute candles (450 windows, Nov 18–23): a side at 2¢ or less reached a 7¢ bid within the next minute 6% of the time, within 3 minutes 10% (300–435 tries). Average −32¢ to −66¢ per $1 depending on fill price. With 5–6 minutes left the hit rate was best (about 11%).

Break-even: a 2¢ → 7¢ win makes about +$2.20 per $1 after fees, and a miss loses about $0.90, so you need roughly 30% wins. Every threshold tried (1–5¢ in, 3–10¢ out, 30 s–3 min holds) came in below that.

Fees assumed: Kalshi taker fee 0.07 × contracts × p × (1−p), rounded up to the cent, on the buy and on the sell (about 7¢ in and 23¢ out per $1 at 2¢ → 7¢). The live order-flow filter can't be backtested (no second-level BTC order book history), and a Kalshi-tape "already bouncing" filter didn't help. Treat SCALP calls as small, lottery-sized bets.

How the meters work
Book pressure: resting bid vs ask size within 0.1% and 0.5% of price. Order flow: over 30 s, (bids added − bids pulled) − (asks added − asks pulled). Taker flow: market buys vs sells filled, 10 s and 60 s. Whales: trades and single orders above the whale sizes over 3 min (big quotes must rest 10 s to count, which filters market-maker flicker). Momentum: 10 s, 1 min and 5 min returns scaled by volatility. Trend: net 5-min move divided by total path travelled. Overall = 15% book, 20% order flow, 25% taker, 10% whales, 20% momentum, 10% trend. Each meter is smoothed over a few seconds.

Entry: P(Up) = Φ(ln(price/target) / (σ·√t) + 0.5·overall/100), with t = minutes left adjusted for Kalshi's 60-second settlement average. GO when P is between 62% and 95% (or 5–38% for Down) and the overall meter agrees; never in the last minute. With Kalshi prices it also needs edge = P − price − fee ≥ the threshold, and the side must cost 5–95¢. "Pay at most" is the highest price that still clears the threshold.

Target: from Kalshi when a proxy is set, otherwise the 60-second average Coinbase price just before the window opened (Kalshi's rule uses the CF Benchmarks index, so it can differ by a few dollars). Type the exact target from Kalshi into the override box for best results.
Probability estimate from public order flow, not a guarantee. 15-minute moves are close to coin flips; size bets small.
